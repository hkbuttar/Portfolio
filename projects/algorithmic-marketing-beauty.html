PROJECT 07

Algorithmic Marketing Optimization in the Beauty Industry

Independent Project 2025 – 2026 Solo

Overview

An end-to-end algorithmic marketing framework analyzing beauty products across Sephora and Ulta, jointly modeling consumer perception, perceived value, price context, and demand dynamics within and across the two retail platforms.

Approach

  • Built resumable, crash-safe Selenium/BeautifulSoup scrapers pulling product and review data from Sephora (Bazaarvoice API) and Ulta (PowerReviews API), then matched brands and products across retailers via fuzzy string matching into a cross-retailer product graph.
  • Ran per-retailer market segmentation with K-Means, Hierarchical Clustering, and Gaussian Mixture Models, and built brand-health pipelines using VADER sentiment and NMF topic modeling to surface delighters, disappointers, and rating-sentiment mismatches.
  • Built content-based recommendation engines (substitutes, complements, trade-up/trade-down) per retailer and a unified cross-retailer engine with category-alias matching and two-pass selection guaranteeing cross-platform representation.
  • Applied PCA-based dual-retailer perceptual mapping, Granger causality testing for cross-platform sentiment spillover, and Difference-in-Differences with propensity score matching to causally estimate the effect of reputation shocks on review velocity, rating, and sentiment by price tier.
  • Surfaced concrete, actionable segments rather than abstract clusters — e.g. Sephora's "Polarizing Problem Children" (lowest ratings, highest variance, elevated helpfulness signaling purchase anxiety) as an intervention queue, and an Ulta segment with 57.3% verified reviewers but only 9.0% verified buyers, flagging visibility built substantially on incentivized reviews. Found that nearly 60% of Sephora's most "disappointed & vocal" products register as neutral-to-positive on Ulta, showing platform signals don't reliably transfer.
