PROJECT 12

Portfolio Strategy Optimization

Independent Project 2026 Solo

Overview

This project asks whether optimization algorithm choice matters for systematic trading strategy selection under operational constraints: does an exact discrete optimizer produce a better, more reproducible selection than a black-box search or a simple greedy rule — and does a better in-sample selection actually translate into better out-of-sample performance? Three strategies (EMA crossover trend following, Bollinger Band mean reversion, and cross-sectional momentum ranking) run across five instruments — SPY, QQQ, BTC-USD, XOM, and GLD, sourced from Yahoo Finance since 2013 — under a constraint of at most K=3 funded strategy-asset pairs from 15 candidates, with no more than one strategy per asset.

Approach

  • Formulated strategy-asset selection as an exact mixed-integer program in OR-Tools CP-SAT, then benchmarked it against Bayesian search and a deterministic greedy ranking rule across 18 rolling rebalances.
  • Sized the selected strategy-asset pairs via convex portfolio optimization in CVXPY, solved with OSQP, CLARABEL, and SCS.
  • Found that CP-SAT never lost the training-window selection objective to either alternative across all 18 rebalances — but greedy tied it exactly in 18/18, showing the problem structure favored a simple heuristic despite the exact solver's theoretical edge.
  • Reported the honest headline finding over the flattering one: despite CP-SAT's in-sample dominance, none of the three optimized methods beat a naive equal-weight benchmark out-of-sample on Sharpe, CAGR, or drawdown.
  • Packaged the pipeline to emit a deployable "decision ticket" — the latest strategy selection and sizing — as actionable output for a portfolio manager deciding which strategies to activate.
